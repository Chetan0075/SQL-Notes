# Business Insights Report

## Project Summary

An analysis was conducted on customer orders, products, and sales transactions to identify customer behavior, product performance, and revenue trends.

---

# Key Findings

## 1. Customer Revenue Analysis

Revenue contribution varies significantly among customers.

Insights:

* A small group of customers contributes a large share of total revenue.
* Repeat customers generate higher lifetime value.
* High-value customers should be targeted for retention programs.

---

## 2. Product Performance Analysis

Product sales are not evenly distributed.

Insights:

* A few products drive most of the sales volume.
* Top-performing products should receive inventory priority.
* Low-performing products should be reviewed for pricing or promotion opportunities.

---

## 3. Category Revenue Analysis

Revenue differs across product categories.

Insights:

* Certain categories generate higher revenue despite lower sales volume.
* High-ticket products significantly impact overall revenue.
* Revenue concentration helps identify strategic business areas.

---

## 4. Customer Purchase Behavior

Analysis of order history reveals purchasing patterns.

Insights:

* Some customers place multiple orders over time.
* Repeat purchasing behavior indicates customer loyalty.
* Retention strategies can increase long-term revenue.

---

## 5. Unsold Products

Products with no recorded sales were identified.

Insights:

* Unsold inventory increases storage and operational costs.
* Marketing campaigns can be targeted toward these products.
* Product portfolio optimization may be required.

---

## 6. Data Quality Findings

Data validation checks were performed.

Observations:

* Duplicate records can distort business metrics.
* Standardized data formatting improves reporting accuracy.
* Database constraints help maintain data integrity.

---

# Recommendations

1. Focus retention efforts on top-spending customers.

2. Maintain sufficient inventory for best-selling products.

3. Review underperforming products and categories.

4. Continuously monitor unsold inventory.

5. Implement data quality checks at the database level.

6. Use Views and Stored Procedures to standardize reporting.

---

# Conclusion

The project demonstrates how SQL can be used to transform raw transactional data into actionable business insights. Through database design, querying, reporting, and optimization techniques, the project simulates a realistic analytics workflow commonly used in enterprise environments.
